# **Psychographic Profile**

## **What They Want**

- To stop losing time and money to manual workarounds and outdated software
- A system that is simple enough that their team will actually use it
- A developer who understands their business - not just their tech stack
- Confidence that the project will be delivered on time and on budget

## **What Keeps Them Up at Night**

**Primary pain:** Operational friction is visibly slowing the business down - stock errors, manual reconciliation, data gaps between locations, reports that take hours to produce manually

**Underlying fear:** Spending money on a dev who disappears, over-builds, or delivers something their team won't adopt

## **Buying Blockers**

| **Blocker**                | **How TechTrinity Counters It**                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "It will be too expensive" | TechTrinity operates from Pakistan - delivering significantly lower rates than US/UK agencies without compromising quality. Transparent scoping upfront. |
| ---                        | ---                                                                                                                                                      |
| "It will take too long"    | Deep operational understanding before building means less rework, faster delivery, and software that fits from day one - no bloat.                       |
| ---                        | ---                                                                                                                                                      |

## **Buying Trigger**

The moment operational friction becomes undeniable - stock errors that cost money, a lost data incident, a new hire exposing how broken the current system is, or the business growing to a point where existing tools simply can't keep up.

# **Behavioral Profile**

**How they research solutions:** LinkedIn, peer referrals from other business owners, Google searches for operational software in their industry

**Who influences the decision:** The owner directly; occasionally a trusted operations manager or accountant

**How they measure success:** Time saved per week, reduction in stock errors, ability to see real-time data across locations, team adoption rate

**Minimum involvement required:** Owner must be willing to spend 2-4 hours in a discovery/scoping phase explaining their operations in detail

**Content lane:** Practical case studies, before/after operational stories, LinkedIn posts that demonstrate domain knowledge in operations/distribution

# **Exclusion Criteria**

- Businesses with more than 100 employees - likely need enterprise ERP, not custom dev
- Prospects who want to own and resell the software IP
- Clients outside US, UK, and Australia at this stage
- Businesses happy with their current setup and not experiencing active operational pain
- Agencies seeking a white-label dev resource at commodity rates

**Churn pattern to watch:** Clients who haven't been through a proper discovery/scoping session - they'll have misaligned expectations on scope and timeline, and are most likely to be dissatisfied regardless of delivery quality

# **Activation Recommendations**

## **LinkedIn Outreach (Primary Channel)**

- Build your outreach list exclusively from Green-tier accounts - wholesale/distribution owners in US/UK/AU, 10-50 employees
- Lead with the pain, not the pitch: reference their operational context before mentioning TechTrinity
- Use your origin story as social proof - "I built a live inventory and warehouse system for a wholesale business that was losing data on USB sticks. It's been running 3 years." That's credibility no agency can fake
- Post content on LinkedIn demonstrating domain knowledge in wholesale operations, inventory challenges, and the cost of bad software - attract inbound before you even need outbound

## **Sales (Discovery & Qualification)**

- Identify Green vs Yellow vs Red in the first 10 minutes: industry, employee count, current tech stack, geography
- Early disqualifier: if they're not feeling active operational friction right now, park them in long-term nurture
- Discovery call must include a deep-dive into their current process before any solution discussion - this is your moat
- Pricing anchor: position against what a US/UK agency would charge for the same scope

## **Customer Success (Once Clients Exist)**

- High-touch onboarding for every client - walk through their workflows before configuring anything
- 30/60/90 day check-ins on adoption rate - simple software should show fast adoption; if it doesn't, investigate early
- Build a referral ask into the 90-day mark for clients who are clearly succeeding - word of mouth in tight-knit industries like distribution travels fast
