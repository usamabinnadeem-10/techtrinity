# 1. Executive Verdict

The ICP is directionally valid, but it should be narrowed from \"small
businesses using outdated software\" to **growing, inventory-heavy
businesses where operational complexity has outgrown the owner's current
tools.** The pain is real in modern markets: manual reconciliation,
spreadsheet dependence, inventory data gaps, delayed reporting, and
disconnected tools remain common even in developed economies.

# 4. Updated Green ICP

import/export, light manufacturing, or inventory-heavy retail business
in the US/UK/AU with 10-75 employees, roughly \$1M-\$15M revenue, 500+
SKUs or recurring stock movement, and operations currently split across
accounting software, spreadsheets, email, warehouse notes, and
disconnected tools. They have a recent trigger: stock errors,
overselling, delayed reports, opening a new warehouse/location, hiring
an ops manager, or spending several hours per week reconciling data
manually.

# 7. Triggers That Move an Account From Interesting to Urgent

-   **New warehouse, branch, or location:** complexity increases and
    spreadsheets break faster.

-   **Hiring operations, warehouse, inventory, or finance/admin staff:**
    a signal that the manual process now needs people to hold it
    together.

-   **Product range expansion:** more SKUs make stock visibility and
    purchasing harder.

-   **Customer/service pressure:** late orders, backorders, or stock
    availability issues create urgency.

-   **Owner bottleneck:** owner is still the only person who can answer
    operational questions.

-   **Data incident:** lost file, wrong report, stock mismatch, or
    duplicate spreadsheet becomes a buying trigger.

-   **Existing SaaS frustration:** they tried off-the-shelf software but
    it forced the business into the wrong process.

# 10. Discovery Questions

-   How do stock movements, sales orders, purchases, and returns flow
    today from start to finish?

-   Where does the team currently check stock availability?

-   Which reports take the most time to prepare manually?

-   What data does the owner not fully trust today?

-   Where does information get duplicated between accounting software,
    spreadsheets, email, and warehouse notes?

-   What changed recently that made this problem more painful?

-   What would success look like 90 days after a new system goes live?

-   What almost stopped you from improving this workflow before?

# 1. Prospecting Principle

**Search accounts first, people second, triggers third, outreach last.**
This ICP is company-first. A person is only worth messaging after the
account shows physical inventory, operational complexity, and a
reachable owner/operator or ops leader.

# 4. Account Qualification Workflow

1.  Open the company page in Sales Navigator and confirm industry,
    headcount, geography, and company description.

2.  Open the company website and confirm they sell/move physical
    products.

3.  Look for product catalog, trade customers, branches, warehouse,
    delivery, quote/order process, and product range.

4.  Find the owner/MD/founder/president and one
    operations/warehouse/finance influencer if possible.

5.  Look for a trigger: hiring, expansion, new location, new product
    range, recent growth, or operational content.

6.  Score the account out of 10 before saving it for outreach.

# 13. If They Reply With Pain

    stock accuracy, order processing, purchasing, reporting, or
    coordination between people/locations.

-   **Ask how they handle it today.** Listen for QuickBooks/Xero, Excel,
    email, WhatsApp, paper, manual reports, or old desktop software.

-   **Quantify the impact.** Ask whether it is mostly annoying or
    starting to cost time/money.

-   **Offer process mapping.** Position a 20-minute call as a way to
    understand the workflow, not a software demo.

# 16. Account Review Checklist Before Messaging

-   Do they sell or move physical products?

-   Do they have enough product/warehouse/order complexity to plausibly
    feel pain?

-   Is there a reachable owner, MD, founder, president, GM, or
    operations leader?

-   Is there a trigger or pain proxy?

-   Can I write a specific, non-creepy first line based on their
    business?

-   Can I clearly explain why I am reaching out without saying \"we
    build software\" first?

# 17. Final Rule

**Every outreach message should sound like: \"I understand the
operational mess behind inventory-heavy businesses.\"**

It should not sound like: \"I am another agency trying to sell
development services.\"
